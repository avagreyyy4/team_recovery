You are locating volleyball huddles in this video clip for a research
study. A huddle is the moment players from BOTH teams (or one team,
depending what's visible) gather together physically after a point ends --
NOT a strategic sideline conference, not a coach talk, not a timeout.

EXACT BOUNDARIES -- this is the most important part of this task. Get the
start and end as tight and accurate as possible; do not pad either
boundary.

- START: the exact instant the point/rally ends -- the specific frame the
  ball becomes dead (hits the floor, goes out, hits the net and stops
  play, a whistle/call ends the rally). This is EARLIER than the moment
  players are already visibly gathering -- a very common mistake is
  reporting a start that is a second or more too late, once the group has
  already started moving together or a high-five/hand-contact is already
  happening. If your candidate start shows players already forming a
  group, already reaching toward each other, or already touching hands,
  that is too late -- rewind to the actual point-ending frame, which is
  usually 1-2 seconds EARLIER than it first looks like it should be.
  Players may still be mid-motion in that exact frame (finishing a swing,
  still airborne from a block/attack, referee mid-signal) -- that is fine
  and expected; the point can end while someone is still physically
  completing the prior action. Do not start earlier than the point-ending
  frame (e.g. mid-rally, before the ball is dead) and do not start later
  (e.g. after players have already begun reaching for each other).

- END: the exact instant BOTH teams begin to separate -- the first visible
  moment the gathered group starts breaking apart and players begin moving
  away from each other. This is EARLIER than the moment they reach their
  next-play/serve-receive positions on the court -- do not extend the
  huddle window to cover players walking back into formation. As soon as
  you see the group start to loosen and disperse (someone turns and
  starts walking away, the tight cluster starts opening up), that is the
  end. If only one side is visible, judge separation from that side alone.
